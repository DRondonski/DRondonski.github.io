About Me

Propulsion, CFD, engineering operations, technical AI, and hands-on build experience brought into one focused profile.

I’m David!

I’m a mechanical engineering student and HOPE/Zell Miller Scholar at the Georgia Institute of Technology, with a minor in biology. My interest in propulsion comes from a broader goal: contributing to the technologies that will enable sustained human presence in space.

What I Work On

My primary technical interest is rocket propulsion. I find the problem compelling because everything matters: fluid mechanics, heat transfer, materials, and manufacturing constraints all converge in a small volume at extreme conditions.

Computational fluid dynamics is my main simulation tool. I use OpenFOAM as my primary solver and have worked with both density-based (rhoCentralFoam) and pressure-based (pimpleFoam, simpleFoam, rhoSimpleFoam) solvers depending on the regime.

My current research connects Python-based geometry generation, machine-learning optimization, CFD, and wind-tunnel validation. I coordinate the workstreams and lead the Python team building a pipeline that generates four-digit NACA airfoils and prepares STL geometry for simulation.

Beyond research, I have managed people and maintenance projects at Amazon and now contribute AEC expertise to the Handshake AI Fellowship by reviewing technical model outputs and helping improve AI understanding of architecture and engineering work.

I chose a biology minor because expanding human presence beyond Earth demands more than propulsion and structures—it requires a thorough understanding of the human body. Physiology, energy systems, and biological limits define what is possible in extreme environments. I am a firm believer that whether it be new spacesuits or genetic modification, the next level needs to be achieved through biological knowledge.

Background

I grew up interested in building things, which eventually led me to mechanical engineering. The deeper I go into thermodynamics, mechanics of materials, fluid mechanics, and design, the more I see how engineers turn difficult fundamentals into systems that move humanity forward. That mix of rigor and creativity is what keeps me committed to the field.

Forward Thinking

I want to keep developing propulsion systems, spacesuit concepts, and life-support technologies for future exploration. My biology minor complements that goal by helping me think about the human limits that engineering systems must protect in extreme environments.

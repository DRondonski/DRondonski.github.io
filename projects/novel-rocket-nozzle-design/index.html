Three-Lobed Rocket Nozzle — First CFD Iteration

A three-leaf-clover nozzle study that produced the correct inverse pressure/Mach pattern, but did not choke the flow.

Overview

I designed this three-leaf-clover rocket nozzle in FreeCAD, converted the geometry to a closed STL, meshed the internal passage, and ran a compressible OpenFOAM case for inspection in ParaView. This page documents the first full CFD iteration honestly: it produced a recognizable converging–diverging response, but it did not establish the intended choked, supersonic solution.

Iteration status

Failed model: the flow accelerates toward the constriction, approaches Mach 1, and then decelerates. Because the throat never establishes a sustained sonic condition, this geometry is not a successful rocket-nozzle solution.

Baseline Case

The animation comes from the actual internal-flow case used for this geometry—not an idealized circular substitute. The mesh retains all three lobes; equivalent diameters below are included only to make the section sizes easier to interpret.

Mach and Pressure

The two animations below show different variables and are labeled explicitly:

  • Mach animation — Ma: dimensionless Mach number. Flow moves from the chamber on the right toward the exit on the left. Mach increases toward the narrow central region.
  • Pressure animation — p: static pressure in pascals. Pressure decreases across the same regions where Mach increases.

That inverse response is the correct qualitative pattern for accelerating compressible flow: Mach rises while static pressure falls. The pressure and Mach fields therefore agree with one another at the trend level.

Why This Iteration Failed

The correct inverse pattern is not enough to make the nozzle successful. The final internal slice shows the central high-speed region remaining roughly around \(M \approx 0.8\text{–}0.9\), followed by a decrease in Mach through the downstream expansion. The surface animation likewise tops out near \(M = 0.85\).

A working converging–diverging nozzle should reach \(M = 1\) at the throat and then continue accelerating to \(M > 1\) through the diverging section. This case instead remains predominantly subsonic: it speeds up in the converging region and slows down again as the area expands. It never establishes a sustained choked-flow plane, so I am treating this result as a failed geometry iteration, not as a performance result or thrust validation.

Next Iteration

My next step is to build a tighter model with a smaller effective throat, then rerun the same baseline conditions so the geometry change can be compared directly. The next design will only advance once the centerline and area-averaged results confirm \(M = 1\) at the throat, continued supersonic acceleration downstream, and a stable mass-flow plateau.
